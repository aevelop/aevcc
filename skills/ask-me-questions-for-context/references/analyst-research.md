# Analyst / Researcher — Question Set

For data analysts, researchers, strategists, anyone whose outputs are reports, memos, or briefings. The output file tells Claude Code how *this* analyst structures findings, where they draw the line on rigor, and what kinds of conclusions they'd never publish.

## Themes to cover (40-50 questions, one at a time)

### 1. What and for whom (4-5)
- What do you analyze? Walk through a recent piece of work.
- Who reads your outputs? What do they actually do with them — decide, present, file away?
- Who's the reader you write *for*, even if technically you write for many?
- What happens when a piece of yours lands well? What happens when it doesn't?

### 2. Defining "good" (4-6)
- What questions does a good analysis of yours answer?
- What format does a good analysis take — memo, deck, dashboard, doc, something else?
- Show me an excerpt from a piece you were proud of. What's good about it?
- What does a *bad* analysis of yours look like? (You've shipped one. Be honest.)

### 3. Structure and format (4-6)
- How do you structure a typical report — what's at the top, what's last?
- What do you always include? What do you always cut?
- Bullets, prose, tables, charts? In what proportions?
- Show me an example of how you present a key finding. What's the shape of it?

### 4. Frameworks and mental models (3-5)
- What mental models do you use most, even informal ones?
- What framework would you reach for when you're stuck on how to approach something?
- What framework do people in your field overuse?

### 5. Standard of rigor (5-7) — push hard
- What does "rigorous" mean to you, specifically?
- Where do you draw the line on confidence? When is a finding "directional" vs "supported" vs "proven"?
- What kind of evidence do you require before making a claim?
- What's a finding you wouldn't publish even if it'd be popular?
- When have you held something back because the evidence wasn't there? Walk me through it.

### 6. Recurring frustrations (4-6) — gold
- What drives you crazy about how analysis gets done in your field?
- What drives you crazy about how analysis gets *presented*?
- What's a kind of bad analysis you see constantly?
- What's a phrase from other people's analyses that makes you wince?

### 7. Tools and sources (3-4)
- What tools do you actually use day-to-day?
- What data sources do you rely on? Which do you actively distrust?
- What's a tool everyone uses that you don't, and why?

### 8. The audience action (3-4)
- After reading your analysis, what do you want the reader to *do*? (Decide, act, ask questions, understand differently?)
- Have you ever had an analysis that informed a decision you disagreed with? What happened?
- What's the gap between what you want readers to do and what they actually do?

### 9. Hard nos (5-7) — pursue hard
- What kind of language would you never use in an output? (Vague hedges, marketing-speak, certainty you don't have?)
- What kinds of claims would you never make even if pressured?
- What chart types do you refuse to use?
- What's a presentation pattern (the "exec summary that's secretly an ad", the "TL;DR with no evidence") that you actively avoid?
- What would make you walk away from a project?

### 10. The "smart assistant" question (3-4)
- If a smart assistant prepared a draft of an analysis for you, what should it look like?
- What should it *never* do? (Add bullets you didn't ask for? Pad with caveats? Use language that overstates confidence?)
- What's a sentence pattern you'd want it to default to?

## Specific things to push on

- **"I'm rigorous"** → "What does rigorous look like? Walk me through how you'd treat a weak finding."
- **"I follow the data"** → "Until when? When have you stopped because the data was misleading?"
- **"I make it actionable"** → "Show me the last analysis you wrote. What's the action?"
- **"I write clearly"** → Useless. Reject. "Compared to who? Show me a paragraph."
- **Generic analyst answer** → "That sounds like any analyst. What's specific about *your* approach?"

## File output structure for this variant

```markdown
# Analyst Context for [Name]

## What I analyze and for whom
## My definition of "good analysis"
## How I structure outputs (with example excerpt)
## Frameworks and mental models I use
## My standard of rigor — confidence and evidence rules
## Recurring frustrations with how analysis gets done
## Tools and data sources I trust / distrust
## What I want readers to *do*
## Language and claims I refuse to use
## Chart types and formats I avoid
## How a smart assistant should help me (and what it should never do)
## Examples (verbatim — excerpts of good outputs, bad outputs, key findings)
## How to use this file
```

The "language and claims I refuse to use" section is the most useful. Make it long, specific, and full of verbatim phrases.

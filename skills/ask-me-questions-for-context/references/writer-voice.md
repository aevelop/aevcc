# Writer Voice — Question Set

For newsletter writers, bloggers, essayists, anyone whose drafts should sound like *them*. The output file makes Claude Code draft in their actual voice instead of a polished AI version of it.

## The rule that makes this work

**80% of voice is rejection.** What the writer never says, never publishes, never agrees with — that's what separates their voice from any other writer in their space. Pursue rejections relentlessly.

## Themes to cover (40-50 questions, one at a time)

### 1. What they write and for whom (3-4)
- What do you write? Where does it get published?
- Who reads it? Not the demographics — what do they actually do with what you write?
- What do you write that you wish more people read? Why don't they?

### 2. Voice at its best (4-6) — demand examples
- Show me a paragraph of yours you're genuinely proud of. (Paste it.)
- What's good about it specifically? Walk me through the sentence-level choices.
- What's a sentence of yours that someone has quoted back to you?
- When does your writing feel most "you" — long essays, short posts, replies to readers, something else?

### 3. Voice when going through the motions (3-5)
- Show me a paragraph you wrote on a bad day. What's flat about it?
- What's the thing you write when you have nothing to say?
- What do you write that you'd retract if you could?

### 4. Phrases that are "you" (3-5)
- What phrases or sentence shapes do you use without thinking?
- What's a word you reach for too often?
- What's a transition or paragraph opener you use repeatedly?

### 5. Words and phrases you hate (5-7) — pursue hard
- What AI-sounding phrases make you cringe? ("delve into", "in the realm of", "it's important to note", etc.)
- What clichés in your space would you never write?
- What words have you actively removed from your vocabulary?
- What's a phrase that signals lazy thinking to you?

### 6. Recurring themes (3-5)
- What topics do you keep coming back to even when you're not trying to?
- What's the throughline across your last 5 pieces?
- What argument do you find yourself making over and over?

### 7. Hard nos (5-7) — pursue hard
- What topics do you refuse to write about?
- What positions would you never take, even if everyone in your space takes them?
- What kind of post would you never publish, even if it would perform?
- What's a piece of advice you'd never give a reader?

### 8. Formatting instincts (4-5)
- Long paragraphs or short? Show me an example of each from your own writing.
- Headers or prose? Lists or sentences? Block quotes?
- How long is your typical piece? How long is too long?
- Where do you start a piece — anecdote, question, claim, scene?

### 9. Writers you steal from (3-4)
- Three writers you admire. For each: what specifically would you steal? (Not "their voice" — a *specific* technique.)
- A writer you used to imitate but grew out of. What changed?

### 10. Feedback patterns (3-4)
- What feedback do you keep getting? (Both compliments and criticisms.)
- What feedback do you keep ignoring? Why?
- What's a thing readers love that you think is overrated about your own work?

## Specific things to push on

- **"I write directly"** → "Show me a paragraph. What makes it direct?"
- **"I'm conversational"** → "Compared to whom?"
- **"I avoid clichés"** → "Name 10 you'd never use."
- **"I like short sentences"** → "Show me five sentences from a recent piece."
- **"I'm authentic"** → Reject. Ask: "What does inauthentic writing look like for you, specifically?"

## File output structure for this variant

```markdown
# Voice Profile for [Name]

## Who I write for
## What my voice sounds like at its best (with 1-2 full paragraph examples)
## Phrases and patterns that are "me"
## Words and phrases I will never use (the rejection list — make this long)
## Topics I keep returning to
## Positions and topics I refuse
## Formatting and structure rules
## Writers I steal from (and what I steal)
## Examples (verbatim — multiple full paragraphs)
## How to use this file
```

Keep the rejection list aggressively long. That section is the difference between drafts that sound like the writer and drafts that sound like AI imitating them.

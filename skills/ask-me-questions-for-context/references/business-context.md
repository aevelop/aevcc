# Business Context — Question Set

For business owners, freelancers, consultants. The output file helps Claude Code with meeting prep, client calls, contractor briefings, proposals, internal decisions.

## Themes to cover (40-50 questions, one at a time)

Use these as themes, not a fixed script. Follow real threads when something interesting surfaces.

### 1. What the business actually is (4-6 questions)
- What do you sell? Who pays you, specifically?
- Describe the business in your own words — not the website version, not the LinkedIn version.
- What problem do customers come to you with? (Get the exact phrasing they use.)
- What's the difference between what you say you do and what customers actually hire you for?

### 2. Customers and segments (3-5)
- Who is the ideal customer? Walk through a recent one — name, what they came for, what they paid.
- Who do you turn down? Why?
- What kind of customer makes you regret saying yes?

### 3. Team and roles (3-5)
- Who works with you — employees, freelancers, contractors, partners?
- Who handles what? Where does the buck stop with you specifically?
- Whose work do you have to redo most often? Why?

### 4. Recurring challenges (5-7) — this is where the gold is
- What meetings do you dread?
- What tasks do you repeatedly procrastinate on?
- What decisions do you struggle with — what makes them hard?
- What do you wish someone else would handle, but only you can?

### 5. Communication style (4-6)
- Show me an actual email you sent this week. (Ask them to paste it.)
- How do you open emails? How do you sign off?
- What do you sound like in meetings — long-winded, terse, big-picture, in-the-weeds?
- What's a phrase you catch yourself using too often?

### 6. Financial setup (3-4)
- How do you track money — what tool, what cadence?
- Who do you report numbers to (board, partner, accountant, self)?
- What financial questions do you actually need to answer regularly?

### 7. Non-negotiables (5-7) — pursue these hard
- What should Claude never suggest about your business?
- What assumption would make you immediately distrust an AI's advice?
- What positions or recommendations are off-limits — even if they'd "work"?
- What kind of email would you never send to a client, no matter what?

### 8. What good output looks like (3-5)
- Last time an assistant or contractor handed you great work — what made it great?
- Show me a deliverable you were genuinely proud of. What's good about it?
- If you had to write a 1-line standard for "good work for this business" — what is it?

## Specific things to push on

- **"I want professional outputs"** → "What does professional look like for you? Show me an email that hit it."
- **"My customers are small business owners"** → "Which kind? Walk me through three real ones."
- **"I'm pretty direct"** → "Show me directness. Paste a paragraph."
- **"My team handles that"** → "Who specifically? What do they actually do day-to-day?"

## File output structure for this variant

```markdown
# Business Context for [Name/Business]

## What the business does (in my words)
## Who pays me (and who doesn't)
## Team structure and ownership
## How I communicate (with examples)
## Recurring challenges I want help with
## Financial standards
## Non-negotiables — never do/suggest these
## What good output looks like (with example)
## Examples & artifacts (verbatim)
## How to use this file
```

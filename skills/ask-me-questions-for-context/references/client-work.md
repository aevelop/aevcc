# Client Work — Question Set

For consultants, agencies, freelancers, project managers. The output file lives in a project folder and tells Claude Code the user's delivery standards, communication norms, and what "done" looks like for *them*.

## Themes to cover (40-50 questions, one at a time)

### 1. The work itself (3-5)
- What kind of work do you do for clients? List the actual deliverables.
- Walk me through a recent project end to end. What did the client get?
- What's a project you'd take again tomorrow? What's one you wouldn't?

### 2. Onboarding and kickoff (4-6)
- What do you need to know about a client before you start work?
- What questions do you ask in kickoff? (Get the actual list.)
- What information do you wish you'd asked for earlier on past projects?
- What's a red flag during onboarding that means the project will go bad?

### 3. Delivery standards (5-7) — push hard
- What does "done" look like for one of your deliverables? Pick one and walk through it.
- What do you always check before sending something to a client?
- What would make you refuse to send a deliverable, even on deadline?
- Show me a deliverable excerpt you were proud of. What's good about it?
- What's the difference between "good enough" and "what you actually send"?

### 4. Client communication (5-7)
- How often do you update clients on a typical project?
- Show me an actual status update or email you sent recently.
- What do you always say in client emails? What do you never say?
- How do you push back on a client request you disagree with? (Demand a real example.)
- How do you deliver bad news to a client?

### 5. Recurring frustrations (4-6) — gold lives here
- What do clients ask for that drives you up the wall?
- What did you have to learn the hard way about working with clients?
- What kind of client request makes you want to fire the client?
- What's a mistake you've made that you'll never make again?

### 6. What makes projects work (4-5)
- Think of two projects: one that went great, one that bombed. In one sentence each, what was different?
- What signals tell you 2 weeks in that a project is going to be excellent vs. disastrous?
- What do you wish clients did more of?

### 7. Hard conversations (4-5)
- How do you handle scope creep? Walk through a specific time.
- How do you handle a missed deadline (yours or theirs)?
- How do you handle disagreements about quality?
- When have you had to fire a client? What was the breaking point?

### 8. Tools and systems (2-4)
- Where do projects live — what tool(s)?
- How do you track tasks, deliverables, time?
- What's a tool you rely on that's non-obvious?

### 9. Non-negotiables (5-7) — pursue hard
- What would you never send to a client without reviewing yourself?
- What kind of deliverable format would you never use? (Bullet-pointed emails? Auto-generated reports? Stock templates?)
- What promises would you never make in a proposal?
- What questions would you never ask a client over email vs. in a call?

### 10. What good output looks like (3-4)
- If a smart assistant prepared something for a client call — what should it look like?
- What's a 1-line standard for "client-ready work" in your shop?

## Specific things to push on

- **"I deliver high-quality work"** → Useless. "Show me a deliverable. What makes it high quality?"
- **"I'm responsive"** → "What's your actual SLA on emails? Show me a thread."
- **"I set clear expectations"** → "Where? In the contract? In kickoff? Read me the actual wording."
- **"I push back when needed"** → "Walk me through the last time you pushed back. What did you say verbatim?"
- **Generic process answer** → "That sounds like any consultant. What's specific about how *you* do it?"

## File output structure for this variant

```markdown
# Client Work Context for [Name]

## What I do for clients
## My onboarding standard (what I need before starting)
## Delivery standards — what "done" looks like
## How I communicate with clients (with example update / email)
## Recurring frustrations and how I handle them
## Scope, deadlines, and hard conversations
## Non-negotiables — never send/say/promise these
## What good client-ready work looks like (with example)
## Tools and where things live
## Examples (verbatim — actual emails, deliverable excerpts)
## How to use this file
```
